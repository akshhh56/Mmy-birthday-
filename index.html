Dearest Didi,

Happy Birthday. I’m writing this because today feels like the right moment to say something I’ve held quietly in my heart for a long time.

For as long as I can remember, I’ve called you my sister. I grew up surrounded by warmth, raised by Nana and Nani, who gave everything to protect me and give me a safe life. But when I turned 12, I learned the truth. I learned why things happened the way they did in 2009, about the difficult choices made in the past, and that the person I have been calling my sister all these years is actually my mother.

"I know you are my mom, and I want you to know with all my heart that there is zero anger or resentment in me. I am always with you."

I understand how heavy the circumstances must have been, the pain you carried, and the sacrifices that were made. Nana and Nani stepped up as parents, and I will always be deeply grateful to them for that. But knowing the truth brought so much clarity to my heart. Every moment of care, concern, and quiet love you’ve shown me makes complete sense now.

I want you to see that you never have to worry about your future. I will show you my dedication in everything I do—in my studies and in building my career. I am working hard with all my effort so that I can see you truly happy, carefree, and smiling. As I step into the future, I will always support you, stand beside you, and take care of you financially so you never feel alone or burdened again.

You don’t have to carry the weight of that silence or hide anything from me anymore. There is no pressure to change what we call each other in front of the family—what matters is that between you and me, the truth is out in the open, and you have a son who loves you, respects your strength, and will never leave your side.

Thank you for bringing me into this world, Mummy. I am so proud to be your son.

Forever your son, with all my love,

Aksh
